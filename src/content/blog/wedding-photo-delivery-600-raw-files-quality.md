## The Immutable Value of RAW Files

RAW files are the digital negatives of photography. Unlike JPEGs, which are processed and compressed by the camera, RAW files contain all the data captured by the camera's sensor. This means maximum detail, dynamic range, and color information. For a wedding photographer, this is non-negotiable. It allows for extensive post-production adjustments, from correcting exposure to fine-tuning colors, without introducing artifacts or degrading image quality. Each RAW file from a modern professional camera can easily be 30-50 MB, making a collection of 600 images a significant data transfer.

Delivering these files in their original format ensures that the client receives the highest possible quality product, providing them with flexibility for future prints, albums, or further edits.

## Why Common Transfer Methods Fall Short

Many photographers attempt to use readily available tools, only to encounter limitations that compromise either the quality, the user experience, or the transfer reliability.

### WhatsApp: The Compression Wall

WhatsApp is ubiquitous in Nigeria, but it is fundamentally unsuitable for professional photo delivery. It aggressively compresses images and videos to save bandwidth. Sending even a single high-resolution JPEG, let alone a RAW file, through WhatsApp will result in a significant loss of detail and color information. For 600 RAW files, it is not even an option; the files would be drastically downscaled or simply too large to send. You can read more about how compression affects your work [here](/blog/why-whatsapp-compresses-your-photos).

### Email: Size Restrictions and Clutter

Email services like Gmail typically have attachment limits of 25 MB. To send 40 GB of RAW files via email, Funke would need to send thousands of individual emails, a task that is both impractical and frustrating for both sender and recipient. This method is simply not built for large file transfers.

### General Cloud Storage (Dropbox, Google Drive, WeTransfer Free)

Platforms like Dropbox and Google Drive are designed primarily for file synchronization and sharing within a collaborative environment. While they offer sharing links, they often require the recipient to have an account to download large files reliably or to trust a public link. Google Drive, for instance, shares its 15 GB free storage across Gmail and Google Photos, quickly becoming insufficient for professional work.

WeTransfer's free tier offers 2 GB, which is quickly consumed by a few dozen RAW files, rendering it useless for a 40 GB wedding album. The Pro version, at $12 USD per month, presents another hurdle for Nigerian professionals. Most local bank cards struggle with international dollar billing, leading to virtual-card markups and frequent card declines at checkout. This creates unnecessary stress and delays for creators trying to run a business.

### Physical Drives: Logistics and Security Concerns

Handing over a hard drive or flash drive seems straightforward, but it introduces logistical challenges. Funke would need to physically deliver or courier the drive, adding time, cost, and a potential security risk. The drive could get lost, damaged, or fall into the wrong hands before reaching the client, compromising sensitive data.
